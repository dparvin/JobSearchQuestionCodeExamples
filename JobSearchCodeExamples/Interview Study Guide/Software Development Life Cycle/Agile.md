# Agile Model Development Methodology
Agile is an iterative and incremental approach to software 
development that emphasizes delivering working software, responding 
to changing requirements, and obtaining frequent feedback from 
stakeholders.

Unlike the Waterfall model, Agile does not assume that all 
requirements can be completely defined before development begins. 
Requirements and solutions can evolve as the team learns more about 
the problem.

Agile is not a single development methodology. It is an approach to 
software development based on a set of principles and values that 
can be applied through methodologies and practices such as Scrum, 
Kanban, and Extreme Programming (XP).

## Core Principles
Agile development emphasizes:

- Delivering working software frequently.
- Responding to changing requirements.
- Frequent collaboration between developers, stakeholders, and customers.
- Continuous feedback.
- Incremental development.
- Continuous improvement of the development process.
- Maintaining a sustainable pace of development.

## Typical Agile Process
A typical Agile development cycle may look like:

```text
Requirements / Backlog
        |
        v
   Prioritization
        |
        v
      Planning
        |
        v
    Development
        |
        v
      Testing
        |
        v
    Demonstration
        |
        v
     Feedback
        |
        +----------+
                   |
                   v
             Next Iteration
```

The exact process varies depending on the Agile methodology being 
used.

## Iterative and Incremental Development
Two important concepts in Agile development are iterative and
incremental development.

Iterative development means that the software is developed through
repeated cycles. Each cycle provides an opportunity to evaluate the
current solution and make improvements.

Incremental development means that functionality is delivered in
smaller pieces rather than waiting for the entire system to be 
completed.

These approaches allow teams to obtain feedback earlier and reduce 
the risk of discovering major problems late in the project.

## Agile vs. Waterfall
The primary difference between Agile and Waterfall is how each 
approach handles change.

Waterfall generally establishes requirements and design early and 
then progresses through sequential phases. Agile expects requirements 
to change and provides opportunities to revisit decisions throughout
development.

| Waterfall                                   | Agile                                      |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Sequential development                      | Iterative development                      |
| Requirements defined early                  | Requirements can evolve                    |
| Large releases                              | Smaller increments                         |
| Feedback often occurs later                 | Frequent feedback                          |
| Changes can be expensive                    | Designed to accommodate change             |
| Testing primarily follows implementation    | Testing occurs throughout development      |
| Progress measured against a predefined plan | Progress measured through working software |

## Advantages
- Adapts to changing requirements.
- Provides working software earlier.
- Allows frequent stakeholder feedback.
- Problems can be discovered earlier.
- Reduces the risk of building a large amount of functionality that
  does not meet user needs.
- Encourages continuous improvement.

## Disadvantages
- Requirements and schedules can be more difficult to predict.
- Requires frequent communication and collaboration.
- Poorly managed Agile processes can result in unclear priorities.
- Teams can accumulate technical debt if development is prioritized
  over maintainability.
- Agile does not eliminate the need for architecture, documentation,
  or planning.

## Agile Does Not Mean "No Planning"
A common misconception is that Agile means development without planning
or documentation.

Agile still requires planning, architecture, requirements, testing,
documentation, and technical decision-making. The difference is that
these activities can be revisited as the team learns more and the
requirements change.

## Common Agile Methodologies

### Scrum
Scrum organizes development into defined iterations called Sprints.
It commonly includes roles such as Product Owner and Scrum Master, 
along with events such as Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint 
Review, and Sprint Retrospective.

### Kanban
Kanban focuses on visualizing work, limiting work in progress, and
improving the flow of work through the development process.

### Extreme Programming (XP)
Extreme Programming emphasizes engineering practices such as
pair programming, test-driven development, continuous integration,
and frequent releases.

## My Experience
When I started at Cougar Mountain Software, the development process 
was primarily based on a Waterfall approach. Requirements and design 
were generally completed before development began.

The team eventually moved toward a more iterative development 
process. This allowed developers and stakeholders to receive 
feedback earlier and made it easier to respond when requirements 
changed or were not fully understood at the beginning of development.

The transition demonstrated an important advantage of iterative
development: requirements do not always become clearer until users 
can see and interact with working software.
