# Water Jug Puzzle (5 and 3 Gallons)

**Purpose:** 

Demonstrates logical reasoning, requirements clarification, and problem-solving under interview conditions.

**Standard water jug puzzle question:** 

You have a 5-gallon jug and a 3-gallon jug. How can you measure exactly 
4 gallons of water?

## Classic Solution

1. Fill the 5-gallon container.
2. Pour into the 3-gallon container. (2 gallons remain.)
3. Empty the 3-gallon container.
4. Pour the 2 gallons into the 3-gallon container.
5. Fill the 5-gallon container again.
6. Pour into the 3-gallon container until it is full (1 gallon).
7. Exactly 4 gallons remain in the 5-gallon container.

## My interview Experience

**My clarifying question:** 

Is there a third container I can use to hold the results?

**Interviewer:**

Sure, there is a third container with 100-gallon capacity.

**My Solution:**

1. Fill the 5-gallon container.
2. Pour into the 3-gallon container. (2 gallons remain.)
3. Pour the 2 gallons into the 100-gallon container.
4. Pour the 3 gallons back into the 5-gallon container.
5. Fill the 5-gallon container again (adding 2 more gallons).
6. Pour into the 3-gallon container until it is full (3 gallon).
7. Pour the remaining 2 gallons into the 100-gallon container, giving you exactly 4 gallons in the 100-gallon container.

## Why My Solution Was Valid

Once the interviewer allowed me to use a third container,
the requirements of the problem changed.

My solution correctly produced exactly four gallons of water in
the 100-gallon container.

However, I later realized the interviewer was probably asking the
well-known two-container puzzle, where the intended solution is to
measure four gallons in the five-gallon container.

## Lessons Learned

- Clarify the problem constraints before solving it.
- State any assumptions your solution depends on.
- Recognize when an interviewer is asking a well-known puzzle with implicit constraints.
- After the interview, learn the classic solution if you didn't know it.
